# Use the time between runs of the crawl, within a window, to make themes and to keep the weave up to date

Status: Proposed
Created: 2026-11-18
Candidate: none (not work too small to need direction: no roadmap covers the crawler, since `theme-weave.md` excludes it in its purpose and `cards.md` and `cross-field-discovery.md` do not name it; the direction is `docs/specs/population.md`, whose non-goal the owner changed on 2026-11-18 for this work; whether a roadmap or a candidate should hold it is the owner's to say)
Specs: docs/specs/population.md ("The idle window", the non-goals, "The crawl plan", "Safeguards", "Scheduling", acceptance criterion 20)

## Goal

Today the loop sleeps after each run of the crawl. The 153 books the crawl has added have a card and no themes, so the theme search, the walk and the weave see only the older books, and `themes-run` is a manual command. When this plan is done, **a run of the crawl ends with an idle window** that makes themes for the books that have none (for at most 60 percent of the window while more books wait for them), then the vectors of the themes (in a batch that fits the time left), then repairs the weave when it is due, and **gives the time back at a deadline** so that the next run starts on time. The window is set by `themes_window_s` in the approved plan (default 600 seconds, 0 is off, at most 3600).

Measured on 2026-11-18, read only, by a script of Python 3.15.0 that printed only counts and seconds, at commit `40fd304` of `main`, from the stored record of the last full run of themes on the owner's library (made on 2026-11-11, 173 books done, 2 failed, with `gemma4:12b`; the record is in the owner's index, which the repository does not hold): a book took a median of 34 seconds, 90 percent took 73 seconds or less, the longest 211; the median book made 12 model calls. The weave was built on 2026-11-13 from 1,805 themes, with no theme waiting (`theme-links status`).

**What increment C was found to need** (measured on 2026-11-18 after increment B, read only, by scripts of Python 3.15.0, at commit `3b6e43e` of `main`, on the owner's library and index; the embedding calls sent made-up sentences of about 120 words, never text of the library): one call to the embedding entry (`qwen3-embedding:4b`, dimension 2560) took 6.8 seconds for 5 texts, 20.9 for 30 and 44.4 for 110, so about 5 seconds and 0.35 seconds a text; a call holds up to 3,906 texts; the library holds 527 books, 181 of them with themes, 1,931 themes; `themevectors.build` reads the library in 1.1 seconds and the old vectors (11,677 of them, in a 150 MB file) in 2.2, and writes its whole file at its end; a full build of the weave from a copy of the file of vectors (1,913 themes) took 5.2 seconds with numpy 2.5.4, so a repair, which does less, takes seconds; 109 texts were waiting to be embedded. The first window of increment B made the themes of 9 books in 300 seconds, each 6 to 49 seconds and 1 to 13 calls, and ended at its deadline. A book's themes are about 60 texts, which cost about as long to embed as the themes took to make. **Consequence:** while books wait for themes the themes fill every window, so vectors made only "after the themes, while time is left" would starve, and the first build, for the whole backlog (9,000 texts or more, about an hour), would fit no window. Hence the split of the window and the batches of this increment.

**What the first windows of increment B showed** (read only, at commit `0c33a50`, from the log of the crawl, the records of the runs and the journal of the oldest book): the second window used its 300 seconds and made no book, and so would every window after it. The oldest book that waited was a whole book of Project Gutenberg, 34 pieces, whose pieces were all answered and kept, so what was left was its checks, which are kept nowhere; a cut book repeats them, and they take longer than a window. The owner made that book by hand with the crawl paused (`themes-run`, 138 calls, 406 seconds of model time, 109 themes, 104 of the calls checks, about 4 seconds each). A book of that size has about 650 texts to embed, which a window cannot embed in whole books. Hence the two further changes of this increment: the answers of the checks are kept as the pieces are, and the batches of vectors are of whole themes and not of whole books.

## Scope

**Changed:**
- `engine/kb/themerun.py`: `run` takes an optional `stop` function; with it a run ends at the stop (see increment A). Without it nothing changes.
- `engine/kb/themecheck.py`, `engine/kb/themeextract.py`: the explicit `stop` passed through them to reach every model call (done in increment A; the `launcher` of this code is the path of the `wb` executable and cannot carry a guard).
- `engine/kb/themevectors.py` (increment C): `build` takes a `stop`, a `max_texts` and a `group`, and a read-only function says how many texts wait; its manual use with none of them is as before.
- `engine/kb/themejournal.py`, `engine/kb/themeextract.py`, `engine/kb/themecheck.py`, `engine/kb/themerun.py` (increment C, a correction of increments A and B): the answers of the checks of a book are kept in a journal as the answers of its pieces are, so that a book whose checks outlast one window is finished by the next without asking for the checks it has answered; the journal is removed when the book is filed or dropped, as the pieces' is. Increment A accepted that a cut book repeats its checks, on the assumption that they fit a window; for a book of 109 themes they do not (see the Goal).
- `engine/kb/crawlidle.py` (increment C, a correction of increment B): the eligibility is brought to the rule of this plan and of the spec, **no themes of any version**. Increment B tested only the version in use (named as a deviation in its Result), so a book converted again (a new version) would have been planned, and a change of the conversion tool would have made the window theme the whole library again, which the rule exists to prevent.
- `engine/kb/crawlplan.py`: the key `themes_window_s` of the section `idle`; `engine/kb/crawlapprove.py`: its line in the preview.
- `engine/kb/crawlrun.py`: the point `idle` after `fetch`, and its place in the registry; `engine/kb/crawlidle.py` (new): the step.
- `engine/kb/crawllog.py`: the steps `idle` (the window's own line), `themes`, `theme_vectors` and `weave` are added to `STEPS`, which refuses any other; `engine/kb/crawlstatus.py` and `engine/kb/crawlreport.py`: what the last window did; `engine/kb/crawlschedule.py`: the text for the scheduler names the project's interpreter.
- Tests in `engine/tests/`, `docs/specs/population.md` (acceptance criterion 20 and the passages already written, kept in step), and `docs/README.md` or the crawl's own how-to if one names the loop line.

**Not changed:** `themes-run`, `theme-vectors` and `theme-links` as commands (the manual use stays as it is); the card step and the judge; `card-index` and `card-vectors` (not run by the window); the full build of the weave; discovery, the fetch and the placement.

## Non-goals

- Making the themes of books that already have themes made with an older prompt or model. A change of the prompt or the model stays `themes-run`'s, run by the owner; the window plans only books that have **no themes at all**.
- A full build of the weave, `card-index`, `card-vectors`, the walk and any search.
- A second model server or a second model for the window: it uses the models that `themes-run` uses (`theme`, `theme-check`).
- Running while the run is refused, stopped or tripped.
- Measuring or removing the cost of switching models on the server between the crawl's models and the themes' models (unmeasured; named under Risks).

## Assumptions and constraints

- The loop runs the project's own interpreter, `engine/.venv/bin/python` (Python 3.15.0, with numpy 2.5.4, checked on 2026-11-18), which the owner changes in the loop line. The system `python3` has no numpy; the window then skips the weave and says so.
- Every model call of the themes goes through `models.call(cfg, name, prompt, launcher=..., root=...)`, where `launcher` is the path of the `wb` executable, so a stop is a function passed down the call path (increment A). The pieces of a book already answered are kept in a journal (`themeextract.PieceJournal`), so a book that is cut is finished later without asking for them again.
- `themerun.plan(store, cfg, book_ids=...)` takes the books to plan, and a run holds at most `cardstate.MAX_ITEMS` books.
- The run already has a time budget of 3600 seconds (`crawlrun.RUN_SECONDS`, the context's `deadline`); the window never goes past it.
- The change is engine code, so the approval of the crawl plan is void until the owner approves again, which is the owner's act at a terminal. A new key in the plan also changes the digest.
- No test uses the network, the owner's models, library or config.

## Completion criteria

1. **Increment A:** `themerun.run(..., stop=f)` makes no call and starts no book once `f()` says to stop; a book cut in the middle stays `waiting`, is charged no attempt, keeps its pieces, and the report says it was stopped; a run with no `stop` is byte for byte as before (the existing tests pass unchanged).
2. **Increment B:** with `themes_window_s = 0` a run makes no themes; with a window and books that lack themes it makes them, ends at the deadline by the injected clock, and finishes a cut book in the next window without asking for its answered pieces again; it plans only the eligible books (active, with a card and a conversion, and no themes at all, so no inactive book, no book without a card or conversion and no book with themes of any version); it makes no model call while the card lock is held by another, and runs through the real lock when it is free; the stop file, a refused or tripped run and the run's own time budget each end or prevent it; a window over 3600 or not a whole number is refused when the plan is read.
3. **Increment C:** **the themes get at most 60 percent of the window** while more books wait for them (all of it when none do, and the share they leave goes to the next part); then the vectors of the themes are made **in a batch that fits the time left** (at 0.35 seconds a text and 5 seconds a call, and 5 seconds for the build's own reading and writing, with a margin of a quarter), **in calls of 40 texts** (about 19 seconds each, so that the stop is looked at about every 20 seconds), the file written holding every theme already embedded and the themes of the batch (**whole themes, with all their texts, never a part of a theme**, so that a book of a hundred themes is embedded over several windows), so that a backlog is worked off over several windows and a build never has to fit all of it; **the answers of the checks of a book are kept as the answers of its pieces are** (a journal of the book, valid only for the same theme text, excerpt, model and prompt version, removed when the book is filed or dropped), so that a book whose checks outlast a window is finished by the next window without asking for the checks it has answered, and a book of any size comes to its end across windows; then the weave is repaired, only when `theme-links status` says it is due and never as a full build, and only when at least 30 seconds are left; a missing numpy skips the weave with a reason in the log and leaves the vectors made; the vector build takes a `stop` (looked at before every embedding call, by the stop file, the deadline and a disk floor), and a cut writes nothing and leaves the old file byte for byte; with nothing waiting the vector step and the repair end at once and say so; whatever time is left unused is given back.
4. **Increment D:** `crawl-status` and `crawl-report` say what the last window did (seconds used, why it ended, books made); the text for the scheduler names the project's interpreter; the spec's passages and acceptance criterion 20 agree with what is built.
5. `wb run code-work check` passes, the tests of the modules changed pass, and the owner has read the Result; after the owner approves the crawl plan again, the first window is looked at in the log.

## Risks and sensitive actions

- **A change of engine code voids the approval of the crawl plan**, and a new key changes its digest. Runs refuse until the owner approves again, at a terminal.
- **Models on the server `ned`, plain http.** The themes send the text of the library's books to `ned` as the cards do. The window adds no other boundary. The owner is asked once whether to run a security review of the window (a model call on library text, a lock shared with the card step, a subprocess-free numpy step).
- **The cost of switching models.** The themes use `gemma4:12b` and `phi4`, the crawl's steps use others; each window may unload the crawl's model, and the first call of the next run loads it again. It is unmeasured; it costs time, not correctness. A window of 0 turns it off.
- **A longer run.** The run lock is held through the window, up to the window or the run's budget. The loop still sleeps between runs; a second run exits quietly while the first holds the lock.
- **A book cut at a call.** It keeps its answered pieces and, from increment C, its answered checks; the worst case is the loss of one call. A book whose pieces and checks are many needs several windows. Until increment C is merged, a book whose checks outlast a window makes no progress in the window (the live case of 2026-11-18, made by hand).
- **A prompt or model change.** The window never remakes themes already made, so it cannot spend the time of a whole library's themes without the owner's word.
- **The vector build and the weave repair rewrite shared files.** The build writes its whole file (150 MB, taken from the temporary file by a rename) once for each window that embeds anything; the repair writes the next generation of the weave and keeps the one before. A kill in the middle leaves the old file in place, as both commands already guarantee. Checked in C.
- **A theme too large for one window's vectors.** The batches are of whole themes, so a book of any size is embedded over several windows. A single theme has about six texts, so the case does not arise in practice; one that did would be left out of the batches, the log would say so, and the owner's `theme-vectors` would make it.
- **Themes made, vectors not yet.** Between the window that makes a book's themes and the one that embeds them, the themes exist and are not found by `theme-search` or linked; the repair waits for vectors. Nothing breaks.
